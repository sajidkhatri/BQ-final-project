First Aid Basics

These are general first aid steps meant to help you act quickly while waiting for professional help to arrive. They are not a substitute for proper medical training — consider taking a certified first aid course if you haven't already.

Always prioritize your own safety before helping others, and call Ambulance (1122) immediately for anything serious.

Bleeding

  • Apply firm, direct pressure to the wound with a clean cloth or bandage.
  • Keep pressure steady for several minutes without lifting to check.
  • If possible, raise the injured area above heart level.
  • Call for medical help if bleeding is heavy or doesn't stop within 10 minutes.

Burns

  • Cool the burn under running water for at least 10 minutes.
  • Do not apply ice, butter, or ointments directly to the burn.
  • Cover loosely with a clean, non-stick cloth.
  • Seek medical attention for large, deep, or facial burns.

Choking

  • Encourage the person to cough forcefully if they can still breathe.
  • If they can't breathe, speak, or cough, perform back blows between the shoulder blades.
  • If that doesn't work and you're trained, perform the Heimlich maneuver.
  • Call emergency services immediately if the airway remains blocked.

Fainting

  • Lay the person flat on their back.
  • Raise their legs slightly above heart level to improve blood flow.
  • Loosen tight clothing around the neck and chest.
  • If they don't regain consciousness within a minute, call for help.

Fractures & Sprains

  • Keep the injured area still and avoid moving the person unnecessarily.
  • Support the area with a splint or padding if available.
  • Apply a cold pack wrapped in cloth to reduce swelling.
  • Seek medical attention, especially if bone is visibly deformed or exposed.

Gas Leakage

  • Do not switch on/off any electrical switches or appliances — this can trigger a spark.
  • Turn off the gas supply valve immediately if it's safe to reach.
  • Open doors and windows to ventilate the area.
  • Evacuate everyone from the area and avoid open flames or smoking.
  • Call the Gas Complaint helpline (1199) from a safe location once outside.

Electric Shock

  • Do not touch the person directly if they're still in contact with the electrical source.
  • Turn off the power supply at the main switch before approaching, if possible.
  • If you can't turn off the power, use a non-conductive object (like a dry wooden stick) to separate the person from the source.
  • Check for breathing and begin CPR if trained and necessary.
  • Call Ambulance (1122) or Electricity Complaint (118) immediately.
